I'm adding this one to the journal because I've been back to it several times, and I wanted to just give it a little callout.

I flew up to Edinburgh on a Friday night with a free weekend ahead of me. On Saturday I journeyed out to [Cramond Island]({{ '/places/cramond-island/' | relative_url }}). On Sunday I visited [Jupiter Artland]({{ '/places/jupiter-artland/' | relative_url }}). This is what I did with the last of the light on Sunday.

I love hiking Arthur's Seat. I try to get up it every time I am in Edinburgh. St Anthony's Chapel sits on a rocky outcrop above St Margaret's Loch, right on the way up. It is about two minutes off the main path, always waiting for anyone who takes the time to wander over to it.

What is left is one tall gable and a stub of another wall, standing on bare rock. It is easy to miss how much is gone. The tower once stood over twelve metres tall, with a spiral staircase inside. Nobody is certain who built it, or exactly when. The detail I like best is that the Pope paid for repairs to it in 1426. There was a holy well on the path just below, one of seven in the park.

After the chapel I carried on up to the top of Arthur's Seat and caught the tail end of the sunset from the summit. Coming back down I got completely turned around, came out on the opposite side of the hill, and ended up walking half way round the base in the dark with a headlamp on. I did not mind in the slightest.

That is really why I keep coming back. It is a 600 year old ruin on a crag in the middle of a capital city, with nobody making a fuss about it. Britain is full of this sort of thing. I do not think I will ever get used to it.

Edinburgh is my favourite city in the world. A ruin like this, sitting quietly above the park at the end of a good weekend, is a decent part of the reason why.
