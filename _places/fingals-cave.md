Fingal’s Cave was my first Weird 300, and I found it by accident. Jillian and I were on Mull with my cousin Peter Chingos and a big hiking party. We were there to climb Ben More, the island’s only Munro and the last of Peter’s 282. We booked a boat trip to the nearby Staffa Island for something to do. The day trip was incredible.

Jillian and I took a Staffa Tours boat from Tobermory, the little harbour town with the painted houses at the top of Mull, and spent the crossing birding from the deck. Then Staffa came up out of the sea. It is a small, flat-topped island that looks like something out of a sci-fi book’s faraway planet.

We learned that the island is basalt that cooled slowly around 60 million years ago and cracked into tall six-sided columns, packed together like pencils in a box. Fingal’s Cave cuts straight into them, and the sea booms and echoes inside. That sound is what sent Mendelssohn home in 1829 to write his Hebrides Overture, at least that’s what Wikipedia says.

I also went for a spontaneous swim in my gym shorts. The water was clear enough to glow green over the submerged rock. The columns rose straight up beside me. It was very cool!

Fast forward four years, and I was leafing through Dave Hamilton’s book and recognized this incredibly distinctive cave. I thought to myself, “Well, Fingal’s Cave was cool, so this book must have most things I’d like.” And here we are.
