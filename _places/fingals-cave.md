Fingal's Cave was our first Weird 300, and we found it by accident. Jillian and I were on Mull with my cousin Peter Chingos, who was there to climb Ben More, the island's only Munro and the last of his 282. The book was not the plan. A boat trip was.

We took a Staffa Tours boat from Tobermory, the little harbour town with the painted houses at the top of Mull, and spent the crossing birding from the deck. Then Staffa came up out of the sea: a small, flat-topped island that looks less like a natural thing than something somebody built.

Nobody built it, of course. The island is basalt that cooled slowly around 60 million years ago and cracked into tall six-sided columns, packed together like pencils in a box. The cave cuts straight into them, and the sea booms and echoes inside. That sound is what sent Mendelssohn home in 1829 to write his Hebrides Overture.

I also went for a swim, in water clear enough to glow green over the submerged rock, with the columns rising straight up beside me.

It was only later, leafing through Dave Hamilton's book, that we realised we had already ticked one off. One down, 299 to go.
