Emma and I took the train up to Cambridge for the day with two of the 300 on the list and a plan to make a proper outing of it. They were both in a park next to a new district on the northwest edge of the city called Eddington.

We came to the Fata Morgana Teahouse first. It sits at the edge of a lake at Brook Leys, part of the drainage system for the new houses. From across the water it reads as a dark cylinder on stilts. Walk round and it resolves into two fat drums of stainless steel mesh with a wavy lid balanced on top.

It is by Wolfgang Winter and Berthold Hörbelt. A fata morgana is a kind of mirage, the one that makes distant ships look like they are floating above the horizon, or turns far-off hills into tall columns. That is a good name for it. In the right light the mesh stops looking solid at all.

I loved that we could go inside. There are no straight lines in there. The mesh walls turn the park into a soft grey blur, so it feels like standing inside a photograph that has not quite finished loading.

Emma promptly declared it boring, so we moved on to the [Pixel Wall]({{ '/places/pixel-wall/' | relative_url }}). Later in the day we got gelato in town, which she called the best part of the whole trip.
