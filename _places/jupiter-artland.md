The book gives every place one photograph, two at most. I have come to enjoy that, and the surprise of everything that surrounds a given weird place. I turn up without really knowing what I am walking into. Jupiter Artland was exactly that. It is a hundred acres of meadow and woodland with art planted all the way through it.


The piece in the book is the one that greets you. It is called Cells of Life, by Charles Jencks. It is the reason you stop walking the moment you come through the gate. Eight grass landforms curve and swirl around four lakes, with a causeway running between them. Jencks built it as a sculpture of mitosis, cells in the act of dividing.

Deeper in the woods are the Weeping Girls, by Laura Ford. There are five of them, small figures in dresses, standing among the trees with their faces hidden. Ford made them faceless on purpose, so you cannot tell whether they are genuinely grief stricken or performing it. They were the standout piece for me. As the father of a daughter, I found them hard to walk past. You end up thinking about how often girls and women are treated badly, and about how much grief they are left carrying in so much of the world. Art with little girls in it lands harder on me than it used to.


I finished my visit with a delicious lunch at the café. I loved how varied the art was. I am learning that I should not be surprised when a place in the Weird Guide turns out to hold far more than the book lets on.
