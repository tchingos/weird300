The book gives every place one or two photographs. But these places are so much more. I have come to enjoy the surprises each location has to offer. Often, I turn up without really knowing what I am walking into. Jupiter Artland was exactly that. It is a hundred acres of meadow and woodland with art planted all the way through it.

The standout art piece, and the photo from the book, is called Cells of Life, by Charles Jencks. Eight grass landforms curve and swirl around four lakes, with a causeway running between them. Jencks built it as a sculpture of mitosis, cells in the act of dividing. Simply put, it's stunning. It's extra unique that you can walk on and around the art piece itself, enjoying the details and the grandeur of it.

Deeper in the woods are the Weeping Girls, by Laura Ford. This piece consists of five small figures in dresses, standing among the trees with their faces hidden. Ford made them faceless on purpose, so you cannot tell whether they are genuinely grief-stricken or performing it. They were a standout for me. As the father of a daughter, I found myself drawn to them and thinking about how often girls and women are treated badly. It made me want to love and spoil my favourite kiddo even more.

I finished my visit with a delicious lunch at the café. All in all, this spot was spectacular. I loved how varied the art was and how I got to be truly "in" it. It was also a great reminder that I should not be surprised when a place in the Weird Guide turns out to hold far more than the book lets on.
