Jillian, Emma, and I went to Eden Dock straight after the Thames Barrier, which is about as different a place as you could pick. It sits in the middle of Canary Wharf, in the strip of water between the towers, right outside the tube station. You come up the stairs and walk straight into it.

A board on the quayside goes back to the Bronze Age. The dock opened in 1806 and took 21 million bricks, a number I read twice. It closed in the 1980s, then came back as Eden Dock in 2024.

The moss figures are the thing to see. There are twenty-one life-size figures standing in the planting and out on the islands, handmade from sphagnum moss and dried flowers by a topiary firm called Agrumi. The piece is called Nature Rising. The figures form a trail that leads you from Jubilee Park down to the water. You keep catching another one out of the corner of your eye.

Eden Dock was worth the stop. The real treat, though, came afterwards. We walked over to B. Bakery nearby and had croissants, which were the highlight of the afternoon for all three of us.
