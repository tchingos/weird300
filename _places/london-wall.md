My visit to the London Wall marks my first deliberate Weird 300. This one was a 15-minute walk from my office. It had me incredibly appreciative of the cultural diversity that I get in London. I really appreciated how the wall was settled in between different buildings, both commercial and residential.

The wall itself went up around 200 AD, when the Romans ringed Londinium in stone. The stretch here formed the north-west corner of the Cripplegate fort. What stands on top of it is a thousand years younger. Bastion 14 is a hollow D shaped tower from the 13th century, raised on the Roman footings, with arrow slits still in its face. You can see any of it only because the Blitz flattened this corner of the City. The wall was dug out afterwards. The Barbican was then built around it rather than over it.

I found myself thinking back to this wall that was built in 200 AD and how it still lasts today. I was feeling a bit existential, thinking about my smallness on this big spaceship Earth that's hurtling around our sun at about 67,000 miles an hour. This was a solo trip, but it has me very excited as I continue to work on this journey.

These were multiple pieces spread over a short distance. It was all walkable too, and right next to the Barbican. One stretch backs onto somebody's private garden, gated off behind the planting. Another curves around a little sitting area with a bench, also gated.

I'm glad the first one I went looking for was this close to my desk. It makes the rest of the list feel less like a project and more like a habit.
