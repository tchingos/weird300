I originally had a work trip to India scheduled, but it got cancelled. With a few spare days on my hands, I decided to visit my favourite city, Edinburgh. Naturally, I opened up my Weird Guide and found a few places to visit. Cramond Island looked particularly cool.

I took a bus from my hotel to a stop near Cramond Beach. I enjoyed breakfast at a little church café called the Gathering Place, which was phenomenal. From there it was a short walk down to the start of the causeway.

Cramond is a tidal island; the walk out only exists for part of the day. You have to go at low tide. Running the whole length of it is a line of concrete pylons, angled and jagged like teeth. They were put there during the Second World War as an anti-boat barrier, to stop torpedo boats slipping up the Forth toward the anchorage and the naval dockyard at Rosyth. They are often described as an anti-submarine barrier, which they are not, because the water here is far too shallow for that. Walking beside them is genuinely strange. They are brutal, repetitive, and somehow beautiful.

The island itself is covered in what the war left behind. There are concrete bunkers all over it, gun emplacements at the northeastern point looking out over the water, and a brick building at the southern end that once held the searchlight that swept the causeway. I spent an hour wandering between them. There is something very cool about being able to walk around inside a piece of history like that, with nothing roped off and nobody telling you where to go.

When I booked the trip, the forecast was just above freezing. What I got was a brisk, sunny day, which in Scotland felt like a gift.
