A few minutes' walk from the [Fata Morgana Teahouse]({{ '/places/fata-morgana-teahouse/' | relative_url }}), on a low bank above a field, is a wall of mirrored steel about fifteen metres long. It curves gently. From a distance it just looks like a metal piece of modern art.

Then you get closer and it comes into focus. The surface is made of narrow horizontal strips, so everything it reflects gets sliced and shuffled. Trees turn into stacks of green blocks. You do not so much see yourself as see a low resolution version of yourself, assembled out of whatever colour you happen to be wearing.

Emma was wearing a bright pink coat. She worked out within about four seconds that she was the most interesting thing available to reflect. We then spent the next ten minutes taking turns posing and running along the wall, watching ourselves in multi-coloured squares.

It is by the same two artists as the teahouse, Winter and Hörbelt. It went up in 2017 as part of the public art for the new neighbourhood. The idea is that it breaks up how you read the landscape, which sounds a bit dry written down. In practice it is a very large toy.

The rest of the day was not weird at all. We went punting on the Cam, where Emma counted the nine bridges we passed under. We wrapped up with an Alice in Wonderland themed escape room, which we solved with some help. It was a great day out, especially for one so close to London.
